## Recommended taxonomy structure
**A question has one or more official topic IDs**, one or more **cross-topic concept group IDs**, and zero or more specific **micro-concept/skill IDs**. Keep College Board science-practice skills, question actions, representations, and misconception tags as separate dimensions. A single item may need many tags; unit, topic, and concept should not be forced into one mutually exclusive dropdown.
**Question-level vs part-level tagging:** For a multi-part FRQ, tag each scoring part or subpart separately and also provide question-level aggregate tags. For MCQ stimulus sets, inherit common context where appropriate without assuming every question assesses all concepts in the shared stimulus.
**Example:** 2026 FRQ Question 2 part A includes molecular geometry, Lewis diagrams, resonance, and formal charge. Other parts of that same FRQ involve net ionic equations, oxidation numbers, electrochemistry, reaction rate graphs, and electrolysis. This is direct evidence that FRQs should be tagged by part, not only by entire question.
**Precision rule:** A concept should be assigned when solving the requested part actually requires it; do not tag all related prerequisite vocabulary appearing only in the passage.

## Separate facets: science practices, actions, representations
College Board officially identifies six science-practice categories. Concept-tagging should not conflate the content assessed with how a student is asked to demonstrate understanding.

- **SP1 Models and Representations:** Describe scientific models and representations, including particulate and macroscopic levels.
- **SP2 Question and Method:** Determine investigable questions, procedures, and methodological effects.
- **SP3 Representing Data and Phenomena:** Construct graphs, diagrams, and particulate models.
- **SP4 Model Analysis:** Analyze, explain, and critique representations and predictions.
- **SP5 Mathematical Routines:** Identify and apply mathematical relationships with units and precision.
- **SP6 Argumentation:** Justify chemical claims with reasoning, models, and evidence.
**Question actions:** identify, calculate, predict, explain, compare, rank, represent/draw, interpret data, construct a model, justify a claim, design/critique an experiment, determine from graph, evaluate error, derive a relationship.
**Input/representation forms:** text-only, Lewis diagram, molecular structure, particulate diagram, table, spectrum, mass spectrum, PES spectrum, concentration-time plot, energy profile, calorimetry data, titration curve, ICE table, cell diagram, electrochemical potential table, chromatogram, calibration curve, reaction equation.

## Explicit AP Exam scope cautions
Exclusion notes below are paraphrased from the CED where applicable, and should guide classifiers away from presenting out-of-scope content as required AP Exam material.

- **1.2:** Mass spectra involving nonsingly charged monatomic species or complex sample mixtures are not assessed as required spectrum interpretations.
- **1.5:** Assignment of four quantum numbers to individual electrons is outside assessed scope.
- **1.7:** Writing configurations for elements that are exceptions to the Aufbau principle is not assessed.
- **2.7:** Derivation or depiction of hybrid orbitals, hybridization involving d orbitals, and molecular orbital diagrams are not assessed.
- **3.3:** Phase-diagram interpretation is outside the assessed scope.
- **3.8:** Colligative properties and calculations of solution molality or solution percent-by-mass/volume are outside scope.
- **4.7:** Rote memorization of solubility rules beyond the College Board specified simple cases is not assessed; terminology of oxidizing/reducing agents is excluded.
- **4.8:** Lewis acid–base theory is excluded; use Brønsted–Lowry concepts.
- **5.5:** Numerical Arrhenius equation calculations are excluded.
- **7.3:** Converting between Kc and Kp is not assessed.
- **8.5:** Numerical concentrations of each species along polyprotic titration curves are excluded; qualitative speciation remains relevant.
- **8.11:** Calculations of salt solubility as a function of pH are excluded; qualitative trends are relevant.
- **9.8:** Assigning positive/negative labels to electrodes is not assessed; anode/cathode and directions remain relevant.
- **9.10:** Focus on qualitative meaning of the Nernst equation and concentration effects; numerical substitution alone is insufficient evidence of understanding.

## Misconception tags supported by the 2025 Chief Reader Report
- **Counts a double bond as two electron domains** (2.7).
- **Assigns hydrogen bonding without identifying a suitably bonded H donor** (3.1, 3.8).
- **Confuses intermolecular attraction with a covalent bond within one molecule** (3.1, 3.3, 3.9).
- **Answers molecular geometry when hybridization is requested, or vice versa** (2.7).
- **Treats molar mass as the full explanation of dispersion forces without electron-cloud polarizability** (3.1).
- **Explains boiling as breaking intramolecular covalent bonds** (3.1, 3.3, 4.4).
